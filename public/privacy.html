Your Photo Frame

Privacy Policy

Effective 4 October 2026

Your Photo Frame is an open-source app that shows your Google Photos as a slideshow on a TV, tablet or phone. It is developed by Padmaraj Bhat. This policy explains what data the app uses, why, where it is kept, and how to remove it. The source code is public at github.com/PadmarajBhat/gphotos-slideshow, so every statement here can be checked against it.

Google user data the app accesses

When you connect a screen, you sign in with Google on your phone and approve access using the Google Photos Ambient API. The app requests the photosambient.mediaitems scope and Google's basic profile scope. It then receives only the photos and videos in the albums you choose for that screen in the Google Photos app. For each item it receives a temporary link to the file, whether it is a photo or a video, its creation time, and its width and height.

The app does not access your other photos or albums, and it does not read your email, contacts or other Google services. It does not store or use your profile information.

How the app uses that data

Solely to show your chosen photos and videos on the screen you connected. The data is not used for advertising, profiling or analytics, is not used to train any model, and is not sold.

Where data is stored

  • Your photos and videos are never stored by the app. They stream directly from Google's servers to your screen.
  • On Google Cloud (Firestore, in the us-central1 region), for each connected screen: your Google sign-in tokens, encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held in Google Secret Manager; the screen's Google Photos device identifier and pairing state; and a cached list of your chosen items' links and details, refreshed about every 50 minutes because Google's links expire after about an hour. Each record is filed under a one-way hash of a random identifier your screen generates, never the identifier itself.
  • On your device (the browser's local storage): your slideshow settings, the last three albums played on that screen including any shared-album link you entered, and the screen's random identifier.

How long data is kept, and how to delete it

  • Disconnect: choose Settings → Disconnect Google Photos. The app revokes its Google access and deletes that screen's stored record and cached list immediately.
  • Revoke from Google: at any time, at myaccount.google.com/permissions. The app loses access straight away, and its stored tokens are discarded the next time the screen checks in.
  • Automatic deletion: records for a screen that has not been used for 90 days are deleted automatically.
  • On your device: clear the browser's site data, or use Settings → Clear recently played.

Sharing

The app does not sell, rent or share Google user data. Google Cloud stores the data described above on the app's behalf. To provide other features, the app also contacts these services, none of which receive data from your Google account:

  • Open-Meteo receives your approximate location to show the weather.
  • freeipapi.com receives your IP address to estimate that location, only if your browser cannot provide one.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim receives map coordinates of demo photos to show place names.
  • api.allorigins.win receives a shared-album link you enter, only if the app's own server cannot load it.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the website.

Turning off Weather or Photo info in Settings stops the related requests.

Limited Use

Your Photo Frame's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Security

All connections use HTTPS. Stored sign-in tokens are encrypted, the encryption key is kept in Google Secret Manager, and the app's server runs under a dedicated account that can only reach its own database and keys.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.
